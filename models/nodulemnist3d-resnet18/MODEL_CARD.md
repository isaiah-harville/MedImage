# Model card: nodulemnist3d-resnet18

## Summary
A 3D ResNet-18 that classifies NoduleMNIST3D volumes (benign, malignant) from 64³ inputs.
No run is published yet, so there are no results to report.

## Intended use
Research only. Not a medical device. Do not use it to diagnose or treat anyone, or on data
other than NoduleMNIST3D volumes.

## Data
- NoduleMNIST3D from MedMNIST v2 (Yang et al., Scientific Data 10:41, 2023,
  doi:10.1038/s41597-022-01721-8), the 64³ archive `nodulemnist3d_64.npz` from Zenodo record
  10519652. Source: LIDC-IDRI (Armato et al., 2011), thoracic CT.
- License: CC BY 4.0 as declared by MedMNIST; the source is CC BY 3.0 with the TCIA Data Usage Policy.
- Split: a random 7:1:2 split by the MedMNIST authors. Train / val / test: 1,158 / 165 / 310
  volumes (`medmnist` metadata). MedMNIST publishes no patient IDs, so leakage cannot be
  checked.
- Labels: malignant or benign, from radiologist malignancy ratings: 1 and 2 are benign, 4 and 5 malignant, and nodules rated 3 are excluded.

## Training
Config: `config.yaml`, generated by `scripts/medmnist_recipes.py` (its docstring explains the
choices). MONAI ResNet-18 with 3D convolutions trained from scratch, AdamW, cosine schedule,
bf16 mixed precision, EMA weights, batch 16, 100 epochs; sized for a 12 GB GPU. Commit,
hardware, and training time: from the run's `run.json`.

## Limitations and biases
The MedMNIST paper does not say the random split keeps a patient's nodules together, so test nodules may share patients with training nodules. Malignancy ratings are readers' judgements, not pathology.
The volumes are 64³ downsamplings, which lose fine detail.

## Reproduce
```bash
uv run opendiagnostics fit -c models/nodulemnist3d-resnet18/config.yaml
```
